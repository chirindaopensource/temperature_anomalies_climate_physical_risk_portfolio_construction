This repository contains an **independent**, professional-grade Python implementation of the arXiv preprint "*Temperature Anomalies and Climate Physical Risk in Portfolio Construction*" (arXiv:2604.11143, first submitted 13 April 2026) by:

*   **Michele Azzone** (Department of Mathematics, Politecnico di Milano)
*   **Carlo Bechi** (Department of Mathematics, Politecnico di Milano)
*   **Gabriele Sbaiz** (Department of Economics, Business, Mathematics and Statistics, University of Trieste)

The paper has no public companion code; everything here was written from the manuscript. The project delivers a dynamic, modular physical climate risk engine. It turns raw monthly continental temperatures into time-varying extreme-event probabilities and a cross-continental dependence structure. It then aggregates them, weighted by each firm's tangible-asset intensity and continental revenue shares, into two portfolio metrics, **Climate Risk Exposure (CRE)** and **Climate Exposure Volatility (CEV)**. Finally it embeds CEV as a third objective, beside expected return and market variance, in a Multi-Objective Particle Swarm Optimisation (MOPSO) that returns a Pareto frontier of long-only, fully invested portfolios.

## Introduction

This project implements the methodology of Azzone, Bechi and Sbaiz (2026). Its core is the Jupyter notebook `temperature_anomalies_climate_physical_risk_portfolio_construction_draft.ipynb`, which has **47 cells (5 markdown and 42 code)** and defines **397 callables: 384 functions, 4 classes and 9 methods**. The code is organised in **40 task cells, Tasks 1–40**. They include two orchestration layers: Task 39, the reporting and reproducibility package, and Task 40, the top-level research pipeline.

**The problem.** The main public measure of physical climate risk, the Notre Dame Global Adaptation Initiative (ND-GAIN) index, is static and country-level. It gives a country a score, but not a multivariate time series of extreme events.

Without such a time series, one cannot estimate how extreme events affect stock returns, nor how events in different regions co-move. Both are needed before one can ask whether physical climate risk can be diversified away. The paper reasons by analogy with Kahn, Ouazad and Yönder (2024), who show that mortgage-backed securities can diversify wildfire risk by limiting the spatial concentration and correlation of the underlying mortgages. The paper applies the same idea to global equity portfolios, using extreme temperature anomalies as the proxy for climate-related events.

The paper reports these headline findings:

- The quadratic-trend logit discriminates extreme months well: AUROC ranges from 0.8380 (Europe) to 0.9396 (Africa) across the six continents (Table 1).
- 17 of 23 sectors have at least one significant $`\gamma_{s,k}`$ (Table 2), and every significant coefficient is negative except one.
- The pooled climate coefficient is $`\Gamma = -0.003`$ with $`p = 0.0096`$ (Equation 10).
- Asia carries the largest climate-normalised weight $`\alpha_k`$ for the MSCI World index.
- CEV falls from about 2010, because several continental probabilities pass $`p = 0.5`$, where the Bernoulli variance $`p(1-p)`$ peaks.
- Portfolios on the three-objective frontier hold on average 22 active positions, against 14 for the mean–variance frontier.
- In the backtest (Table 9), the (1/3, 1/3, 1/3) tilt reaches the highest CAGR, 33.54 %. The minimum-variance portfolio has the smallest maximum drawdown, 11.47 %.

**What the codebase lets you do.** Each group of tasks is a module:

- **Validate** twelve raw input tables against typed schemas and invariants, and cleanse and reconcile them with auditable row ledgers (Tasks 1–3).
- **Build the climate signal:** baseline moments, standardised anomalies, 2σ Bernoulli events, per-continent quadratic-trend logits, and their AUROC/AUPR diagnostics and restricted-sample robustness check (Tasks 4–10).
- **Model dependence:** the empirical Pearson (phi) matrix, the time-averaged marginals and the Fréchet–Hoeffding admissibility audit (Tasks 11–13).
- **Estimate the return regressions:** a survivorship-controlled 120-firm universe, asset intensity, revenue shares, sector excess returns, the climate-augmented CAPM with Newey–West errors, and the sector and global fixed-effects panels (Tasks 14–21).
- **Compute exposures and metrics:** climate-normalised weights, CRE, CEV, the asymptotic decomposition, the strategy comparison and the correlation stress test (Tasks 22–27).
- **Estimate and condition the moments:** rolling five-year expected returns and covariance, with Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage to a condition number below 1,000 (Tasks 28–29).
- **Run the MOPSO:** Algorithm 1 with vectorised objectives and Euclidean simplex projection, the anchor frontier at 2020-01-01, and its diagnostics (Tasks 30–32).
- **Sweep the hyperparameters:** the 27-cell × 10-seed grid behind Tables 5–7 and the selection of the Table C.10 configuration (Tasks 33–34).
- **Backtest:** 64 monthly re-optimisations from 2020-01 to 2025-04, eleven strategies, Table 9 and Figure 10 (Tasks 35–36).
- **Reproduce Appendix B:** the CRE-variant frontier and its figures (Tasks 37–38).
- **Report:** the artifact inventory, equation verification, judgment-call reconciliation and a SHA-256-stamped reproducibility bundle (Task 39).
- **Drive everything** through one staged interface, `run_research_pipeline` (Task 40).

> **Reproduction status: read this first.**
> - The repository ships **no data**. The temperature series is public (Our World in Data). The MSCI World constituents, total returns, tangible assets, geographic-segment revenues and index membership are commercial data that you must license yourself.

> - The embedded usage example generates a synthetic world with Faker and NumPy. It was run end to end against the draft notebook in a sandbox, in smoke mode (`CLIMATE_RISK_SMOKE_TEST=1`). ## Theoretical Background

The study combines climatology, probability theory (dependence between Bernoulli variables), financial econometrics, portfolio theory and evolutionary multi-objective optimisation. The equation numbers below are those of the manuscript.

### 1. Standardised Anomalies (Equation 1)

The data are Our World in Data's monthly mean temperatures from 1940-01 to 2025-04 for six continents: North America, South America, Europe, Africa, Asia and Oceania. Antarctica is excluded. For each continent $`k`$ and calendar month $`m`$, the mean $`\mu_{k,m}`$ and standard deviation $`\sigma_{k,m}`$ are taken over the 1960–1990 baseline. The raw temperature $`T_{k,t}`$ becomes

```math
Z_{k,t} = \frac{T_{k,t} - \mu_{k,m}}{\sigma_{k,m}}, \qquad\text{(1)}
```

where $`m`$ is the calendar month of $`t`$.

### 2. Extreme-Event Indicator (Equation 2)

An extreme event is a standardised anomaly above 2:

```math
B_{k,t} = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } Z_{k,t} > 2 \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}. \qquad\text{(2)}
```

Under a Gaussian, this is the upper tail $`1 - \Phi(2)`$, about the top 2.28 % of observations.

### 3. Logistic Regression with a Quadratic Trend (Equation 3)

The time-varying probability $`p_{k,t}`$ of an extreme event is modelled continent by continent:

```math
\mathrm{logit}(p_{k,t}) = \ln\left( \frac{p_{k,t}}{1 - p_{k,t}} \right) = \beta_{0,k} + \beta_{1,k}t + \beta_{2,k}t^2. \qquad\text{(3)}
```

$`\beta_{1,k}`$ captures the linear progression of risk, and $`\beta_{2,k}`$ whether the frequency of extremes is accelerating or decelerating. Table 1 of the paper reports the discrimination of the fit estimated on 1940–2025:

| Continent | AUROC | AUPR | AUPR Random |
|---|---|---|---|
| Africa | 0.9396 | 0.8031 | 0.2060 |
| Asia | 0.9067 | 0.7312 | 0.2225 |
| Europe | 0.8380 | 0.3776 | 0.0943 |
| North America | 0.8947 | 0.6100 | 0.1429 |
| Oceania | 0.8830 | 0.3215 | 0.0564 |
| South America | 0.8754 | 0.5227 | 0.1341 |

Figure 2 of the paper compares, for Europe and North America, the fit on the restricted 1940–2019 training sample (used for portfolio selection) with the fit on the full 1940–2025 sample. The restricted curve closely tracks the full-sample curve.

### 4. Dependence and Admissible Correlations (Equations 4–7)

The dependence between continents is measured by the Pearson correlation of $`B_{i,t}`$ and $`B_{j,t}`$. The paper reports values that are all positive and lie between 0 and 0.48. Because the indicators are Bernoulli, their correlation is bounded. For random variables $`X_1, X_2`$ with distribution functions $`F_1, F_2`$ and $`U \sim \mathrm{Unif}([0, 1])`$, the Fréchet–Hoeffding theorem gives

```math
\mathrm{corr}(F_1^{-1}(U), F_2^{-1}(1 - U)) \le \mathrm{corr}(X_1, X_2) \le \mathrm{corr}(F_1^{-1}(U), F_2^{-1}(U)). \qquad\text{(4)}
```

For $`X_1 \sim \mathrm{Bern}(p_1)`$ and $`X_2 \sim \mathrm{Bern}(p_2)`$ this yields the closed forms

```math
\rho_{\min} = \frac{\max(0, p_1 + p_2 - 1) - p_1 p_2}{\sqrt{p_1(1 - p_1)}\sqrt{p_2(1 - p_2)}} \qquad\text{(5)}
```

```math
\rho_{\max} = \frac{\min(p_1, p_2) - p_1 p_2}{\sqrt{p_1(1 - p_1)}\sqrt{p_2(1 - p_2)}}. \qquad\text{(6)}
```

Since $`p_{k,t}`$ changes over time, the bounds are evaluated at the time-averaged probability over the 1940–2020 training horizon:

```math
\bar{p}_k = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T \hat{p}_{k,t}. \qquad\text{(7)}
```

> **Pairwise versus joint admissibility.** The bounds (5)–(6) are pairwise. For a multivariate Bernoulli distribution of more than two variables, further constraints apply (Huber and Marić, 2015, 2019). These constraints generally have no closed form beyond four variables, and this study has six. The pairwise bounds are therefore necessary, not sufficient, conditions.

### 5. Return Regressions (Equations 8–10)

Sector returns $`R_{s,t}`$ are market-cap-weighted averages of the total returns of MSCI World constituents. Banks, financials and insurers are excluded. For every sector $`s`$ and continent $`k`$, the climate-augmented CAPM is estimated over 2004–2025 with Newey–West HAC standard errors:

```math
R_{s,k,t} - R_{f,t} = \alpha_{s,k} + \beta_{MKT,s,k}(R_{MKT,t} - R_{f,t}) + \gamma_{s,k}B_{k,t} + \epsilon_{s,k,t}. \qquad\text{(8)}
```

The sector panel adds month and continent fixed effects:

```math
R_{s,k,t} - R_{f,t} = \alpha_s + \beta_{MKT,s}(R_{MKT,t} - R_{f,t}) + \gamma_s B_{k,t} + \text{FE}_{\text{month}} + \text{FE}_{\text{continent}} + \epsilon_{s,k,t}. \qquad\text{(9)}
```

The global panel pools all sectors and continents:

```math
R_{s,k,t} - R_{f,t} = \alpha + \beta_{MKT}(R_{MKT,t} - R_{f,t}) + \Gamma B_{k,t} + \text{FE}_{\text{month}} + \text{FE}_{\text{continent}} + \epsilon_{s,k,t}. \qquad\text{(10)}
```

The implementation estimates Equations (9) and (10) under one shared run identifier. `run_two_scope_panel_fe_driver` enforces this, and each scope cross-checks the other's run identifier and data fingerprint.

> **Registered divergence (panel standard errors).** The manuscript names Newey–West HAC errors for Equation (8) and is silent on the panel estimator. The code's headline errors for Equations (9)–(10) are Driscoll–Kraay; stacked-row Newey–West is kept as a sensitivity diagnostic (`JC-driscoll-kraay`). P-values for Table 3 and $`\Gamma`$ need not match the paper even on identical data.

### 6. Vulnerability and Localisation (Equations 11–12)

A firm's physical vulnerability is its asset intensity:

```math
AI_i = \frac{\text{Tangible Assets}_i}{\text{Revenue}_i}. \qquad\text{(11)}
```

The paper reports the highest sector average for Real Estate (10.6) and the lowest for Retailers (0.6). With $`S_{i,k}`$ the revenue that firm $`i`$ earns in continent $`k`$, the climate-normalised portfolio weight of continent $`k`$ is

```math
\alpha_k(\mathbf{w}) = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N w_i \cdot AI_i \cdot S_{i,k}}{\sum_{k=1}^K \sum_{i=1}^N w_i \cdot AI_i \cdot S_{i,k}}. \qquad\text{(12)}
```

### 7. Climate Risk Exposure and Climate Exposure Volatility (Equations 13–16)

CRE is the expected climate exposure of the portfolio at time $`t`$:

```math
\text{CRE}(\mathbf{w}, t) = \mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{k=1}^K \alpha_k(\mathbf{w}) B_{k,t}\right] = \sum_{k=1}^K \alpha_k(\mathbf{w}) \cdot p_{k,t}. \qquad\text{(13)}
```

CEV is the variance of that exposure:

```math
\text{CEV}(\mathbf{w}, t) = \mathrm{Var}\left(\sum_{k=1}^K \alpha_k(\mathbf{w}) B_{k,t}\right) = \sum_{k=1}^K \alpha_k^2 \mathrm{Var}(B_{k,t}) + 2\sum_{k < j} \alpha_k \alpha_j \mathrm{Cov}(B_{k,t}, B_{j,t}), \qquad\text{(14)}
```

with $`\mathrm{Var}(B_{k,t}) = p_{k,t}(1 - p_{k,t})`$ and

```math
\mathrm{Cov}(B_{k,t}, B_{j,t}) = \rho_{k,j}\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(B_{k,t})\mathrm{Var}(B_{j,t})} = \rho_{k,j}\sqrt{p_{k,t}(1 - p_{k,t})p_{j,t}(1 - p_{j,t})}. \qquad\text{(15)}
```

Substituting gives the closed form that the code evaluates:

```math
\text{CEV}(\mathbf{w}, t) = \sum_{k=1}^K \alpha_k^2 p_{k,t}(1 - p_{k,t}) + 2\sum_{k < j} \alpha_k \alpha_j \rho_{k,j}\sqrt{p_{k,t}(1 - p_{k,t})p_{j,t}(1 - p_{j,t})}. \qquad\text{(16)}
```

The first term is Herfindahl-like: it measures the geographic dispersion of risk and, for uniform probabilities, is smallest when $`\alpha_k = 1/K`$. The second term is systemic: correlated extremes in continents where the portfolio is heavily exposed raise its volatility. The decline of CEV after about 2010 follows from the Bernoulli variance $`p(1-p)`$, which peaks at $`p = 0.5`$. Once several $`p_{k,t}`$ exceed 0.5 and keep rising, the variance falls even though the expected frequency of extremes stays high.

### 8. Asymptotics (Equations 17–18)

With $`\alpha_k = 1/K`$, $`p_k = p`$ and $`\rho_{k,j} = \rho`$, the idiosyncratic term vanishes as the number of regions grows:

```math
\sum_{k=1}^K \left(\frac{1}{K}\right)^2 p(1 - p) = \frac{1}{K} p(1 - p) \;\to\; 0, \qquad\text{(17)}
```

but the systemic term does not:

```math
2\sum_{k < j} \frac{1}{K^2}\rho p(1 - p) = 2 \frac{K(K - 1)}{2}\frac{1}{K^2}\rho p(1 - p) = \left(1 - \frac{1}{K}\right)\rho p(1 - p) \;\to\; \rho p(1 - p). \qquad\text{(18)}
```

Finer geographic granularity removes local dispersion but cannot remove the risk that comes from the correlation of climate events.

### 9. The Tri-Objective Programme and MOPSO (Equations 19a–19c, Algorithm 1)

The universe is $`N = 120`$ stocks, stratified by headquarters region as in Table 4: 54 North America (45 %), 30 Europe (25 %), 30 Asia (25 %) and 6 Oceania (5 %). The programme is

```math
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\mathbf{w}} \quad & F(\mathbf{w}) = [f_1(\mathbf{w}), f_2(\mathbf{w}), f_3(\mathbf{w})]^\top && \text{(19a)} \\
\text{s.t.} \quad & \sum_{i=1}^{120} w_i = 1, && \text{(19b)} \\
& w_i \ge 0, \quad \forall i = 1, \dots, 120, && \text{(19c)}
\end{aligned}
```

with $`f_1(\mathbf{w}) = -\mathbf{w}^\top \mathbf{r}`$, $`f_2(\mathbf{w}) = \mathbf{w}^\top \mathbf{\Sigma}\mathbf{w}`$ and $`f_3(\mathbf{w}) = \text{CEV}(\mathbf{w})`$. The covariance is conditioned with Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage so that its condition number is below 1,000, and the CEV objective is evaluated in matrix form rather than with loops.

The MOPSO of Algorithm 1 (Appendix C.2) keeps an external repository of non-dominated portfolios. It partitions the objective space into a grid of $`N_{grid}`$ cells per dimension, with the bounds inflated by $`dc = (s_{\max} - s_{\min}) \cdot \alpha`$. Leaders are selected with pressure $`\beta`$, and repository members are deleted with pressure $`\gamma`$. Each particle's velocity is updated as

```math
vel[i] \gets \omega\, vel[i] + c_1 r_1 (gbest\_pos - pos[i]) + c_2 r_2 (pbest\_pos[i] - pos[i]),
```

and the mutation probability at iteration $`t`$ is

```math
p_m = \left(1 - \frac{t}{\text{Iter} - 1}\right)^{1/\mu}.
```

Table C.10 fixes the configuration: $`N_{pop} = 500`$, $`\text{Iter} = 300`$, $`N_{rep} = 400`$, $`N_{grid} = 10`$, $`\alpha = 0.1`$, $`\beta = \gamma = 1`$, $`\mu = 2`$, $`\omega = 0.8`$, $`c_1 = c_2 = 1.5`$, and weight bounds $`[0, 1]`$. The configuration was chosen with the hypervolume (convergence and diversity; higher is better) and spacing (uniformity along the frontier; lower is better) of Tables 5–7.

> **Registered labelling inconsistency ($`c_1`$, $`c_2`$).** The paper's prose calls $`c_1`$ the social coefficient, which pulls the particle towards a repository leader, and Algorithm 1 multiplies $`c_1`$ by $`(gbest\_pos - pos[i])`$. Table C.10 labels $`c_1`$ "Personal learning coefficient" and $`c_2`$ "Global learning coefficient". The implementation follows Algorithm 1's update rule (`JC-c1c2-label`). Because both values are 1.5, the choice has no numerical effect.

### 10. Frontier Diagnostics and the Backtest (Equation 20, Tables 8–9)

A weight below $`10^{-3}`$ is treated as zero when counting active positions. The structural distance between two frontier portfolios is the continuous Jaccard distance

```math
d_J(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y}) = 1 - \frac{\sum_i \min(x_i, y_i)}{\sum_i \max(x_i, y_i)}. \qquad\text{(20)}
```

The frontier is also sliced into variance intervals, and each slice is projected onto the (CEV, expected return) plane with a parabolic fit (Figure 9).

In the backtest (2020-01-01 to 2025-04-30), the frontier is re-optimised on the first business day of each month. Expected returns are the mean monthly returns over the preceding five years; the covariance uses daily returns over the same five years; and the probabilities come from the month before rebalancing. Besides the market-cap-weighted and equally weighted benchmarks and the three corner portfolios (minimum variance, minimum CEV, maximum expected return), portfolios are selected by min–max normalising the three objectives and minimising their convex combination with weights $`(a, b, c)`$. The six tilts of Table 8 are (1/3, 1/3, 1/3), (0, 1/2, 1/2), (1/2, 1/4, 1/4), (1/4, 1/2, 1/4), (1/4, 1/4, 1/2) and (1/2, 1/2, 0). Performance is measured by CAGR, the Sharpe and Sortino ratios with $`R_f = 0`$, maximum drawdown, and the realised exposures r-CRE (sample mean) and r-CEV (sample variance). **No transaction costs** are modelled, as the paper states.

> **Registered divergence (Sharpe and Sortino annualisation).** The code computes Sharpe as $`12 \cdot \text{mean}(r) / \text{std}(r)`$ and Sortino with the same factor. The factor 12.0 comes from `config.yaml`, where the study-inputs specification chose it (`JC-md-sharpe_sortino_annualisation_factor`). The conventional annualisation multiplies by $`\sqrt{12}`$, so the reproduced ratios are $`\sqrt{12} \approx 3.46`$ times the conventional annualised values that the paper's Table 9 magnitudes suggest. The embedded usage example explains how to switch to $`\sqrt{12}`$.

## Features

- **40-task modular architecture.** Each task is one notebook cell with its own top-level orchestrator. Tasks 1–29 and 31–39 are pipeline stages; Task 30 is the MOPSO engine they reuse; Task 40 is the staged interface.
- **Configuration-driven design.** `config.yaml` has 21 top-level sections and carries the same data tree as `study_config.json`. It carries a SHA-256 checksum of its own tree, computed by Task 1's `compute_config_checksum`.
- **Single-namespace notebook design.** All callables live in one executed notebook. The pipeline resolves each stage orchestrator by name in that namespace; no module is loaded from disk.
- **Staged interface with fail-fast manifests.** Every call validates its inputs, deep-copies and injects the configuration, times each stage and writes a manifest. A failing stage aborts with its cause chained, or is recorded when `fail_fast=False`.
- **Two-scope panel fixed-effects proof.** The sector panel (Equation 9) and the global panel (Equation 10) run under one shared `run_id`, and each checks the other's data fingerprint.
- **Survivorship-controlled universe.** Firms must be MSCI World constituents at both 2020-01-01 and 2025-01-01. The 120-firm frozen order is sorted by `firm_id` and sealed with a SHA-256 digest that later stages verify.
- **Vectorised CEV.** Equation (16) is evaluated for all periods, or for a whole swarm, as one matrix contraction.
- **Duchi simplex projection.** Particle positions are repaired by Euclidean projection onto the probability simplex (Duchi et al., 2008), so Equations (19b)–(19c) always hold.
- **Seed discipline.** Each MOPSO run has its own PCG64 stream. Each sweep cell is replicated with 10 independent seeds (1–10), and the protocol also fixes an estimator seed of 42.
- **Hardware ratio.** Runtimes are compared with the paper's 217.39 s per month through $`h = 217.39 / T_{\text{anchor}}`$, where $`T_{\text{anchor}}`$ is the anchor MOPSO runtime. The ratio is recorded as pending, not fabricated, when no anchor measurement exists.
- **Six integrity gates in Task 39** (fixtures, coverage, readme, register, environment, bundle). Reproduction mismatches against the paper are reported in `verdict_counts` but never gate `passed`.
- **Judgment-call register.** `config.yaml` holds 113 entries: **89** code-level judgment calls and 24 `JC-md-` decisions carried over from the study-inputs specification. Task 39 reconciles the code-level entries in both directions, line by line, against the `JC-` markers in the notebook's own task cells.
- **Reproducibility bundle.** `environment.json` records package versions and SHA-256 digests; the bundle also holds the seed state, a `manifest.json` and a `tar.gz` archive.
- **Smoke mode.** `CLIMATE_RISK_SMOKE_TEST=1` shrinks the swarm, the monthly backtest swarm and the sweep (two small cells with two seeds), so that every stage can be exercised quickly. Smoke-mode results are not paper-scale results.

**Staging contract.** Each stage receives only the keyword arguments given for it in `stage_inputs`. The function does **not** chain outputs from one stage into the next. A run from raw data therefore calls `run_research_pipeline` once per stage (`stages=[N]`), in canonical order, and builds each stage's inputs from the deliverables already returned. The embedded usage example does exactly this. Task 30 is never a stage: its engine runs inside Tasks 31, 33, 34, 37 and 38.

**Manifest.** The manifest records the stage order, the wall time of each stage, the failed stages and the paper's reference runtime of 217.39 s. When Task 31 has run, it also records the hardware ratio $`h`$ and the timings scaled by $`h`$.

**Errors.** A type-contract breach raises `TypeError`. A rejected stage list or rejected inputs raise `ValueError`. A failing stage under `fail_fast=True` raises `RuntimeError` with chained context. A stage orchestrator or Task 1 symbol missing from the namespace raises `ImportError`.

**Stage 39.** `orchestrate_task_39` takes a keyword-only `notebook_path`: the notebook whose task cells supply the task sources for the bundle README and the judgment-call reconciliation. If it is `None`, Stage 39 reads the notebook file named by `NOTEBOOK_FILENAME` in the working directory.

### **Step 1: Synthetic Data Generation (the twelve raw tables)**

**Methodology.** One seeded PCG64 stream drives a single, internally consistent synthetic world:

1. **Climate (item i).** The monthly continental surface-air temperature $`T_{k,t}`$ for 1940-01 to 2025-04 is built from three parts: a hemisphere-aware seasonal cycle; a warming signal that is flat before 1970 and quadratic afterwards (about +1.6 °C by 2025); and AR(1) anomalies. The 2-σ exceedances of Equation (2) therefore become more frequent over time, as in the manuscript's Figure 2.
2. **Returns (items ii–iv, xi).** Firm USD total returns follow the climate-augmented CAPM of Equation (8) with a sector factor:
   $`R_{i,t} = R_{f,t} + \beta_i (R_{MKT,t} - R_{f,t}) + f_{s(i),t} + \gamma_i \sum_k S_{i,k}\,(B_{k,t} - \bar B_k) + \varepsilon_{i,t}`$.
   Here $`B_{k,t} = \mathbf 1\{Z_{k,t} > 2\}`$ is computed from the synthetic temperatures with the 1960–1990 baseline of Equation (1), so the climate signal the pipeline later estimates is genuinely present. Local-currency returns are obtained by inverting the FX identity $`1 + r^{USD}_t = (1 + r^{loc}_t)\,(fx_t / fx_{t-1})`$. The market factor has a 0.7 % monthly mean, 4.3 % volatility and the 2008 and 2020 drawdowns. The risk-free rate is the ACT/360 accrual of a realistic 1-month T-bill path.
3. **Firms (items v–x).** There are 160 MSCI-World-style firms: 142 non-financial candidates and 18 banks and insurers, which Section 2.4 excludes. Eleven candidates leave the index at the 2023 review. The frozen universe is the top 54/30/30/6 by anchor market cap within North America, Europe, Asia and Oceania (Table 4). Faker supplies legal names and tickers. Asset intensities reproduce Section 3.1's benchmarks (Real Estate ≈ 10.6, Retailers ≈ 0.6). Asian firms are tilted towards asset-heavy manufacturing, so that, as the manuscript reports, **Asia has the largest climate-normalised weight** $`\alpha_k`$ (Equation 12).
4. **Calendar (item xii) and daily returns.** The trading calendar is the `global_intersection` of NYSE, LSE, TSE and ASX. Each monthly return is split across its trading days with a Brownian bridge on log returns, which includes a common market shock so that daily returns co-move.

```python
# ==========================================================================
```

```python
# ==========================================================================


def _month_grid(start: str, end: str) -> pd.DatetimeIndex:
    """
    Build a first-of-month grid between two dates, both inclusive.

    Parameters
    ----------
    start : str
        First month (ISO date, any day of the month).
    end : str
        Last month (ISO date, any day of the month).

    Returns
    -------
    pd.DatetimeIndex
        Month-start timestamps, datetime64[ns], tz-naive.

    Raises
    ------
    ValueError
        If ``start`` is after ``end``.
    """
    # Parse both boundaries and normalise them to their month start.
    first: pd.Timestamp = pd.Timestamp(start).to_period("M").to_timestamp()
    # Normalise the end boundary identically.
    last: pd.Timestamp = pd.Timestamp(end).to_period("M").to_timestamp()
    # Reject an inverted window before building anything.
    if first > last:
        # Raise naming both boundaries.
        raise ValueError(f"start={start} is after end={end}.")
    # Return the inclusive month-start grid.
    return pd.date_range(first, last, freq="MS")


def generate_temperature_panel(
    rng: np.random.Generator,
    snapshot_date: str = "2025-05-15",
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Generate ``df_temperature_raw``: monthly continental mean temperature.

    Purpose
    -------
    Simulates the OWID continental surface-air-temperature series
    :math:`T_{k,t}` that enters Equation (1),
    :math:`Z_{k,t} = (T_{k,t} - \\mu_{k,m(t)}) / \\sigma_{k,m(t)}`.

    Processes
    ---------
    1. A deterministic seasonal cycle per continent (hemisphere-aware
       phase) sets the climatology :math:`\\mu_{k,m}`.
    2. A warming signal is flat before 1970 and grows quadratically
       afterwards (about +1.6 degC by 2025), which makes the 2-sigma
       exceedance probability of Equation (2) rise over time, as in
       the manuscript's Figure 2.
    3. AR(1) anomalies (phi = 0.5) with continent-specific scale add
       month-to-month persistence.
    4. Provenance columns required by the revised schema are attached.

    Parameters
    ----------
    rng : np.random.Generator
        Seeded generator driving every random draw.
    snapshot_date : str, optional
        Download date recorded in ``source_snapshot_date``.

    Returns
    -------
    pd.DataFrame
        One row per (continent, year_month), 1940-01 to 2025-04.
    """
    # Validate the generator type so a legacy RandomState is rejected.
    if not isinstance(rng, np.random.Generator):
        # Raise naming the received type.
        raise TypeError(f"rng must be np.random.Generator, got {type(rng)}.")
    # Build the 1940-01..2025-04 monthly grid.
    months: pd.DatetimeIndex = _month_grid(TEMPERATURE_START, TEMPERATURE_END)
    # Express time in fractional years for the warming trend.
    years: np.ndarray = (
        months.year.to_numpy() + (months.month.to_numpy() - 1) / 12.0
    )
    # Warming is zero before 1970 and quadratic afterwards (degC).
    warming: np.ndarray = (
        1.6 * np.clip((years - 1970.0) / 55.0, 0.0, None) ** 2
    )
    # Collect one frame per continent.
    frames: List[pd.DataFrame] = []
    # Simulate each continent independently.
    for continent in CONTINENTS:
        # Unpack the continent's climatology parameters.
        mean_c, amp_c, peak_m = CLIMATOLOGY[continent]
        # Seasonal cycle: mean + amplitude * cos(2*pi*(m - peak)/12).
        seasonal: np.ndarray = mean_c + amp_c * np.cos(
            2.0 * np.pi * (months.month.to_numpy() - peak_m) / 12.0
        )
        # Draw Gaussian innovations for the AR(1) anomaly process.
        shocks: np.ndarray = rng.normal(
            0.0, ANOMALY_SD[continent], months.size
        )
        # Allocate the AR(1) anomaly path.
        anomaly: np.ndarray = np.empty(months.size)
        # Initialise the path at its first innovation.
        anomaly[0] = shocks[0]
        # Recurse a_t = 0.5 a_{t-1} + sqrt(1 - 0.25) e_t (unit-variance AR).
        for t in range(1, months.size):
            # Apply the AR(1) recursion with variance-preserving scaling.
            anomaly[t] = 0.5 * anomaly[t - 1] + math.sqrt(0.75) * shocks[t]
        # Assemble the observed temperature T_{k,t}.
        temperature: np.ndarray = seasonal + warming + anomaly
        # Store the continent's frame.
        frames.append(pd.DataFrame({
            "continent": continent,
            "year_month": months,
            "calendar_month": months.month.astype("int64"),
            "temperature_celsius": np.round(temperature, 3),
        }))
    # Stack the six continents into one long panel.
    df: pd.DataFrame = pd.concat(frames, ignore_index=True)
    # Flag the inclusive 1960-01..1990-12 baseline window (372 rows each).
    df["in_baseline_window"] = df["year_month"].between(
        pd.Timestamp(BASELINE_START), pd.Timestamp(BASELINE_END)
    )
    # Record the spatial aggregation convention of the source series.
    df["spatial_aggregation_method"] = "area_weighted_mean"
    # Record the physical measurement convention.
    df["measurement_convention"] = "surface_air_temperature_2m"
    # Record the download snapshot for reproducibility.
    df["source_snapshot_date"] = pd.Timestamp(snapshot_date)
    # Mark every synthetic observation as complete.
    df["data_quality_flag"] = "valid"
    # Enforce the declared dtypes explicitly.
    df = df.astype({
        "continent": "object", "calendar_month": "int64",
        "temperature_celsius": "float64", "in_baseline_window": "bool",
    })
    # Return the panel sorted on its primary key.
    return df.sort_values(["continent", "year_month"]).reset_index(drop=True)
```

```python
def simulate_bernoulli_events(df_temperature: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Recover B_{k,t} from the synthetic temperatures for the DGP only.

    The synthetic return generator needs the realised extreme-event
    indicators so that returns genuinely load on them (Eq. 8). This
    helper applies Equations (1)-(2) with the 1960-1990 baseline; the
    pipeline recomputes them independently in Tasks 4-6.

    Parameters
    ----------
    df_temperature : pd.DataFrame
        Output of :func:`generate_temperature_panel`.

    Returns
    -------
    pd.DataFrame
        Wide frame indexed by year_month with one 0/1 column per
        continent: B = 1{Z > 2}.
    """
    # Validate the input type.
    if not isinstance(df_temperature, pd.DataFrame):
        # Raise naming the received type.
        raise TypeError("df_temperature must be a pandas DataFrame.")
    # Select the baseline rows used for the month-specific moments.
    base: pd.DataFrame = df_temperature[df_temperature["in_baseline_window"]]
    # Estimate mu_{k,m} and sigma_{k,m} (sample sd, ddof=1).
    moments: pd.DataFrame = base.groupby(
        ["continent", "calendar_month"]
    )["temperature_celsius"].agg(mu="mean", sigma="std").reset_index()
    # Attach the moments to every observation.
    merged: pd.DataFrame = df_temperature.merge(
        moments, on=["continent", "calendar_month"], how="left"
    )
    # Equation (1): Z = (T - mu) / sigma.
    merged["Z"] = (
        (merged["temperature_celsius"] - merged["mu"]) / merged["sigma"]
    )
    # Equation (2): B = 1{Z > tau}, tau = 2.
    merged["B"] = (merged["Z"] > 2.0).astype("int64")
    # Pivot to a month x continent matrix.
    return merged.pivot(index="year_month", columns="continent", values="B")
```

```python
def generate_firm_skeleton(rng: np.random.Generator) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Draw the static attributes of every synthetic MSCI World firm.

    Parameters
    ----------
    rng : np.random.Generator
        Seeded generator.

    Returns
    -------
    pd.DataFrame
        One row per firm with firm_id, region, sector, currency,
        country, beta, climate loading, leaver flag and 2004 market cap.
    """
    # Collect one record per firm.
    rows: List[Dict[str, Any]] = []
    # Keep a running firm counter for stable identifiers.
    counter: int = 0
    # Iterate over the four investable regions of Table 4.
    for region, n_candidates in CANDIDATES_PER_REGION.items():
        # Tilt Asian firms three-to-one towards asset-heavy manufacturing.
        tilt: np.ndarray = np.array([
            3.0 if (region == "Asia" and s in ASIA_MANUFACTURING_SECTORS)
            else 1.0 for s in SECTORS
        ])
        # Sample the non-financial sectors with the regional tilt.
        sectors: List[str] = list(
            rng.choice(SECTORS, size=n_candidates, p=tilt / tilt.sum())
        )
        # Guarantee every sector appears at least once in North America.
        if region == "North America":
            # Overwrite the first 23 draws with one firm per sector.
            sectors[: len(SECTORS)] = list(SECTORS)
        # Append the region's financial firms after the candidates.
        sectors += list(rng.choice(FINANCIAL_SECTORS,
                                   size=FINANCIALS_PER_REGION[region]))
        # Choose which candidates leave the index before 2025.
        leavers: set = set(rng.choice(n_candidates,
                                      size=LEAVERS_PER_REGION[region],
                                      replace=False).tolist())
        # Materialise each firm of the region.
        for j, sector in enumerate(sectors):
            # Advance the firm counter.
            counter += 1
            # Draw the reporting currency from the regional pool.
            currency: str = str(rng.choice(REGION_CURRENCIES[region]))
            # Store the static record.
            rows.append({
                "firm_id": f"MSCIW{counter:04d}",
                "region": region,
                "sector": sector,
                "is_financial_sector": sector in FINANCIAL_SECTORS,
                "currency": currency,
                "country": str(rng.choice(CURRENCY_COUNTRIES[currency])),
                "beta": float(rng.normal(1.0, 0.22)),
                # Firm-specific climate sensitivity (negative on average,
                # consistent with the sign pattern of Tables 2-3).
                "gamma": float(rng.normal(-0.008, 0.004)),
                "is_leaver": (
                    sector not in FINANCIAL_SECTORS and j in leavers
                ),
                # Lognormal 2004 market cap (USD), larger for North America.
                "cap_2004": float(np.exp(rng.normal(
                    24.2 if region == "North America" else 23.6, 0.9))),
            })
    # Return the skeleton frame.
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)


def generate_fx_rates(rng: np.random.Generator) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Generate ``df_fx_rates_raw``: month-end USD per unit of local currency.

    The table carries the revised-schema columns (date, from_currency,
    to_currency, rate, source, quote_type) and the two keys the Task 2
    validator declares for the FX grid (``currency``, ``year_month``).

    Parameters
    ----------
    rng : np.random.Generator
        Seeded generator.

    Returns
    -------
    pd.DataFrame
        One row per (currency, month), 2004-01..2025-04.
    """
    # Build the monthly grid of the return window.
    months: pd.DatetimeIndex = _month_grid(RETURNS_START, RETURNS_END)
    # Collect one frame per currency.
    frames: List[pd.DataFrame] = []
    # Simulate a driftless log random walk per currency.
    for ccy, (level, vol) in FX_START_LEVEL.items():
        # Draw monthly log changes.
        steps: np.ndarray = rng.normal(0.0, vol, months.size)
        # Anchor the first month at the historical 2004-01 level.
        steps[0] = 0.0
        # Cumulate the log path and exponentiate to a USD-per-unit rate.
        path: np.ndarray = level * np.exp(np.cumsum(steps))
        # Store the currency's frame.
        frames.append(pd.DataFrame({
            "date": months + pd.offsets.MonthEnd(0),
            "from_currency": ccy,
            "to_currency": "USD",
            "rate": path,
            "source": "WM/Reuters",
            "quote_type": "wm_reuters_4pm_london",
            "currency": ccy,
            "year_month": months,
        }))
    # Return the stacked FX table.
    return pd.concat(frames, ignore_index=True)


def generate_market_factor(rng: np.random.Generator) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Generate ``df_market_factor_raw``: MSCI World net total returns (USD).

    Returns have a 0.7% monthly mean and 4.3% volatility, with the
    2008-09/10 and 2020-03 drawdowns injected at historical magnitude.

    Parameters
    ----------
    rng : np.random.Generator
        Seeded generator.

    Returns
    -------
    pd.DataFrame
        One row per month, 2004-01..2025-04.
    """
    # Build the monthly grid.
    months: pd.DatetimeIndex = _month_grid(RETURNS_START, RETURNS_END)
    # Draw Student-t(5) shocks scaled to 4.3% monthly volatility.
    shocks: np.ndarray = (
        rng.standard_t(5, months.size) * 0.043 / math.sqrt(5 / 3)
    )
    # Add the 0.7% monthly drift R_MKT = mu + shock.
    r_mkt: np.ndarray = 0.007 + shocks
    # Inject the historical crisis months (approximate magnitudes).
    for month, value in {"2008-09-01": -0.12, "2008-10-01": -0.19,
                         "2020-03-01": -0.13, "2020-04-01": 0.11}.items():
        # Overwrite the month's return.
        r_mkt[months.get_loc(pd.Timestamp(month))] = value
    # Assemble the frame with the revised-schema provenance fields.
    return pd.DataFrame({
        "year_month": months,
        "index_name": "MSCI World",
        "R_MKT_t": r_mkt.astype("float64"),
        "currency": "USD",
        "index_return_type": "net_total_return",
        "index_version": "MSCI_World_NDR_2025Q2_release",
        "vendor": "MSCI_direct",
    })


def generate_riskfree() -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Generate ``df_riskfree_raw``: monthly 1-month T-bill holding returns.

    The monthly decimal rate is the simple ACT/360 accrual of the
    annual yield, R_f,t = y_t * days_in_month / 360. Because the stored
    value is a MONTHLY holding-period rate, the declared annualisation
    factor is 12 (the "monthly" family the Task 2 validator matches
    against the detected magnitude of R_f,t). Section 5 sets R_f = 0 for
    Sharpe/Sortino; this series feeds only the excess returns of
    Equations (8)-(10).

    Returns
    -------
    pd.DataFrame
        One row per month, 2004-01..2025-04.
    """
    # Build the monthly grid.
    months: pd.DatetimeIndex = _month_grid(RETURNS_START, RETURNS_END)
    # Look up the annual yield (decimal) for each month's calendar year.
    annual: np.ndarray = np.array(
        [TBILL_ANNUAL_PCT[m.year] / 100.0 for m in months]
    )
    # Apply the ACT/360 simple accrual for each month's day count.
    r_f: np.ndarray = annual * months.days_in_month.to_numpy() / 360.0
    # Assemble the frame.
    return pd.DataFrame({
        "year_month": months,
        "R_f_t": r_f.astype("float64"),
        "instrument": "T-BILL-1M",
        "compounding_convention": "simple",
        "day_count_basis": "ACT/360",
        "annualisation_factor": 12.0,
    })
```

```python
def draw_label_shares(
    rng: np.random.Generator, region: str,
) -> Dict[str, float]:
    """
    Draw one firm's segment-label revenue mix with a home bias.

    Parameters
    ----------
    rng : np.random.Generator
        Seeded generator.
    region : str
        HQ region selecting the label scheme.

    Returns
    -------
    Dict[str, float]
        Label -> revenue share, summing to one.
    """
    # Fetch the region's reporting labels (home label first).
    labels: Tuple[str, ...] = REGION_SEGMENT_LABELS[region]
    # Concentrate the Dirichlet on the home label (home bias).
    alpha: np.ndarray = np.array([6.0] + [1.0] * (len(labels) - 1))
    # Draw the revenue mix.
    shares: np.ndarray = rng.dirichlet(alpha)
    # Return the label -> share mapping.
    return dict(zip(labels, shares.tolist()))


def label_shares_to_continents(
    label_shares: Mapping[str, float],
) -> np.ndarray:
    """
    Convert label shares to the six-continent share vector S_{i,k}.

    Parameters
    ----------
    label_shares : Mapping[str, float]
        Label -> revenue share.

    Returns
    -------
    np.ndarray
        Shape (6,), continents in ``CONTINENTS`` order, summing to one.
    """
    # Start from a zero exposure vector.
    s: np.ndarray = np.zeros(len(CONTINENTS))
    # Allocate each label's share through the mapping weights.
    for label, share in label_shares.items():
        # Distribute the label's share across its mapped continents.
        for continent, weight in SEGMENT_MAP[label].items():
            # Accumulate S_{i,k} += share * allocation_weight.
            s[CONTINENTS.index(continent)] += share * weight
    # Return the continental share vector.
    return s
```

```python
def generate_trading_calendar(
    start: str = "2015-01-01", end: str = "2025-04-30",
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Generate ``df_trading_calendar_raw`` for the global-intersection market.

    A date is a trading day only when NYSE, LSE, TSE and ASX are all
    open. The union of their closures is approximated by weekends, US
    federal holidays, 2-3 January (TSE), 26 December (LSE/ASX) and 31
    December (TSE).

    Parameters
    ----------
    start, end : str
        Inclusive calendar window; it must cover the five-year lookback
        before the first rebalance (2020-01) and the backtest horizon.

    Returns
    -------
    pd.DataFrame
        One row per calendar date for market ``global_intersection``.
    """
    # Enumerate every calendar date in the window.
    dates: pd.DatetimeIndex = pd.date_range(start, end, freq="D")
    # Obtain US federal holidays over the window.
    us_holidays: pd.DatetimeIndex = (
        USFederalHolidayCalendar().holidays(start, end)
    )
    # Identify the fixed-date closures of the other three exchanges.
    fixed: np.ndarray = (
        ((dates.month == 1) & np.isin(dates.day, (1, 2, 3)))
        | ((dates.month == 12) & np.isin(dates.day, (25, 26, 31)))
    )
    # A trading day is a weekday that is not a holiday on any exchange.
    is_trading: np.ndarray = (
        (dates.dayofweek < 5) & ~dates.isin(us_holidays) & ~fixed
    )
    # Build the calendar frame.
    cal: pd.DataFrame = pd.DataFrame({
        "date": dates,
        "market": "global_intersection",
        "is_trading_day": is_trading,
    })
    # Keep only the trading days for first/last-day detection.
    trading: pd.DataFrame = cal[cal["is_trading_day"]]
    # Group trading days by calendar month.
    by_month = trading.groupby(trading["date"].dt.to_period("M"))["date"]
    # Flag the first trading day of each month (rebalance date, Sec. 5).
    cal["is_month_first_business_day"] = cal["date"].isin(by_month.min())
    # Flag the last trading day of each month (valuation date).
    cal["is_month_last_business_day"] = cal["date"].isin(by_month.max())
    # Record the calendar source.
    cal["calendar_reference"] = "vendor_consolidated"
    # Return the calendar.
    return cal
```

#### Step 1.6: Returns, universe, metadata, membership, fundamentals, segments and daily returns

```python
def simulate_firm_returns(
    rng: np.random.Generator,
    firms: pd.DataFrame,
    b_wide: pd.DataFrame,
    s_true: pd.DataFrame,
    df_mkt: pd.DataFrame,
    df_rf: pd.DataFrame,
    fx_wide: pd.DataFrame,
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Simulate ``df_firm_total_returns_raw`` from the Equation (8) DGP.

    Purpose
    -------
    Each firm's USD total return follows the climate-augmented CAPM of
    Equation (8) with a sector factor:
    R_i,t = R_f,t + beta_i (R_MKT,t - R_f,t) + f_s(i),t
    + gamma_i sum_k S_i,k (B_k,t - mean_t B_k) + eps_i,t,
    so returns genuinely load on the realised extreme events B_k,t of
    the synthetic temperature panel, weighted by each firm's true
    continental revenue exposure S_i,k (the logic of Equation 12).

    Parameters
    ----------
    rng : np.random.Generator
        Seeded generator.
    firms : pd.DataFrame
        Firm skeleton (firm_id, sector, currency, beta, gamma, cap_2004).
    b_wide : pd.DataFrame
        Month x continent matrix of realised B_k,t.
    s_true : pd.DataFrame
        Firm x continent true revenue shares S_i,k.
    df_mkt : pd.DataFrame
        Market factor table (R_MKT_t).
    df_rf : pd.DataFrame
        Risk-free table (R_f_t).
    fx_wide : pd.DataFrame
        Month x currency USD-per-unit rates (USD column = 1).

    Returns
    -------
    pd.DataFrame
        Firm x month panel with local and USD returns, caps and FX.
    """
    # Build the month grid of the return window.
    months: pd.DatetimeIndex = _month_grid(RETURNS_START, RETURNS_END)
    # Align the event matrix to the return months and CONTINENTS order.
    b_ret: np.ndarray = (
        b_wide.reindex(months)[list(CONTINENTS)].to_numpy(float)
    )
    # Demean events by their in-window frequency (surprise component).
    b_surprise: np.ndarray = b_ret - b_ret.mean(axis=0, keepdims=True)
    # Draw one monthly common shock per sector (sector factor, 2%).
    sector_shock: Dict[str, np.ndarray] = {
        s: rng.normal(0.0, 0.02, months.size)
        for s in sorted(set(firms["sector"]))
    }
    # Pull the market return vector.
    r_mkt: np.ndarray = df_mkt["R_MKT_t"].to_numpy()
    # Pull the risk-free vector aligned to the same months.
    r_f: np.ndarray = df_rf["R_f_t"].to_numpy()
    # Collect one frame per firm.
    frames: List[pd.DataFrame] = []
    # Simulate each firm's USD total return and market-cap path.
    for firm in firms.itertuples(index=False):
        # Firm exposure vector S_i,k in CONTINENTS order.
        s_i: np.ndarray = s_true.loc[firm.firm_id].to_numpy()
        # Climate term: gamma_i * sum_k S_i,k (B_k,t - mean B_k).
        climate: np.ndarray = firm.gamma * (b_surprise @ s_i)
        # Idiosyncratic Student-t(6) noise with 6% monthly volatility.
        eps: np.ndarray = (
            rng.standard_t(6, months.size) * 0.06 / math.sqrt(1.5)
        )
        # Equation (8) DGP plus the sector factor.
        r_usd: np.ndarray = (
            r_f + firm.beta * (r_mkt - r_f) + sector_shock[firm.sector]
            + climate + eps
        )
        # Keep returns inside the [-0.5, 0.5] sanity band.
        r_usd = np.clip(r_usd, -0.45, 0.45)
        # Month-end market cap: compound price return (2.4%/yr payout).
        cap: np.ndarray = firm.cap_2004 * np.cumprod(1.0 + r_usd - 0.002)
        # Fetch the USD-per-local rate path of the firm's currency.
        fx_path: np.ndarray = fx_wide[firm.currency].to_numpy()
        # FX gross change fx_t / fx_{t-1} (first month has no lag: 1).
        fx_ratio: np.ndarray = np.concatenate(
            ([1.0], fx_path[1:] / fx_path[:-1])
        )
        # Invert the FX identity: 1 + r_local = (1 + r_usd) / fx ratio.
        r_local: np.ndarray = (1.0 + r_usd) / fx_ratio - 1.0
        # Store the firm's monthly rows.
        frames.append(pd.DataFrame({
            "firm_id": firm.firm_id,
            "year_month": months,
            "total_return": r_local,
            "market_cap_usd": cap,
            "currency": firm.currency,
            "total_return_usd": r_usd,
            "fx_rate_used": fx_path,
            # Validator aliases: the local-currency return and FX rate
            # under the names the Task 2 Step 2 contract reads.
            "total_return_local": r_local,
            "fx_rate": fx_path,
        }))
    # Stack the monthly firm panel (item ii).
    df: pd.DataFrame = pd.concat(frames, ignore_index=True)
    # Record the vendor total-return methodology.
    df["corporate_action_adjustment_method"] = "vendor_total_return"
    # Record net dividend treatment, consistent with the net index.
    df["dividend_treatment"] = "net"
    # No synthetic return is ever revised.
    df["is_revised"] = False
    # A never-revised row has no revision date.
    df["revision_effective_date"] = pd.NaT
    # Return the panel.
    return df


def freeze_universe(
    firms: pd.DataFrame, df_returns: pd.DataFrame,
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Build ``df_universe_120_raw``: the Table 4 stratified universe.

    Eligible firms are non-financial (Section 2.4) and constituents at
    both 2020-01-01 and 2025-01-01 (survivorship control). Within each
    region the largest firms by the market cap known at the anchor
    date (the 2019-12 month-end value) fill the 54/30/30/6 quotas.

    Parameters
    ----------
    firms : pd.DataFrame
        Firm skeleton with region, financial and leaver flags.
    df_returns : pd.DataFrame
        Monthly panel carrying market_cap_usd.

    Returns
    -------
    pd.DataFrame
        Exactly 120 selected firms.

    Raises
    ------
    RuntimeError
        If the quotas cannot be filled.
    """
    # Parse the analysis anchor date.
    anchor: pd.Timestamp = pd.Timestamp(ANALYSIS_ANCHOR)
    # The latest cap known at 2020-01-01 is the 2019-12 month-end cap.
    cap_known: pd.Series = df_returns[
        df_returns["year_month"] == anchor - pd.offsets.MonthBegin(1)
    ].set_index("firm_id")["market_cap_usd"]
    # Eligible = non-financial and a constituent at both anchors.
    eligible: pd.DataFrame = firms[
        ~firms["is_financial_sector"] & ~firms["is_leaver"]
    ].copy()
    # Attach the anchor market cap for ranking.
    eligible["market_cap_usd_at_anchor"] = (
        eligible["firm_id"].map(cap_known)
    )
    # Rank by anchor market cap within region (1 = largest).
    eligible["rank_by_market_cap_within_region"] = (
        eligible.groupby("region")["market_cap_usd_at_anchor"]
        .rank(ascending=False, method="first").astype("int64")
    )
    # Keep the top-quota firms of each region (54/30/30/6).
    sel: pd.DataFrame = eligible[
        eligible["rank_by_market_cap_within_region"]
        <= eligible["region"].map(REGIONAL_QUOTAS)
    ].copy()
    # Build the frozen universe table (item ix).
    universe: pd.DataFrame = pd.DataFrame({
        "firm_id": sel["firm_id"],
        "region": sel["region"],
        "regional_quota_target": (
            sel["region"].map(REGIONAL_QUOTAS).astype("int64")
        ),
        "rank_by_market_cap_within_region": (
            sel["rank_by_market_cap_within_region"]
        ),
        "selection_method": "top_cap_within_region",
        "selection_seed": pd.array([pd.NA] * len(sel), dtype="Int64"),
        "analysis_anchor_date": anchor,
        "market_cap_usd_at_anchor": sel["market_cap_usd_at_anchor"],
        "inclusion_reason": "selected",
    })
    # Order by region then rank.
    universe = universe.sort_values(
        ["region", "rank_by_market_cap_within_region"]
    ).reset_index(drop=True)
    # Verify the Table 4 quota total of exactly 120 firms.
    if len(universe) != 120:
        # Raise naming the achieved count.
        raise RuntimeError(f"universe has {len(universe)} firms, not 120.")
    # Return the frozen universe.
    return universe


def build_metadata_and_membership(
    firms: pd.DataFrame,

    """
    Build ``df_firm_metadata_raw`` and ``df_index_membership_raw``.

    Faker supplies realistic legal names and unique tickers; membership
    is an annual 1 January snapshot panel 2004-2025 in which the
    "leaver" firms are deleted at the 2023 index review.

    Parameters
    ----------
    firms : pd.DataFrame
        Firm skeleton.

    Returns
    -------
    Tuple[pd.DataFrame, pd.DataFrame]
        (metadata, membership).
    """
    # Build firm metadata with Faker names and tickers (item vii).
    metadata: pd.DataFrame = pd.DataFrame({
        "firm_id": firms["firm_id"],
        "firm_name": [fake.unique.company() for _ in range(len(firms))],
        "ticker": [
            fake.unique.lexify("????").upper() for _ in range(len(firms))
        ],
        "sector": firms["sector"],
        "sector_classification_scheme": "TRBC",
        "sector_classification_version": "TRBC_2023",
        "country_of_domicile": firms["country"],
        "hq_continent": firms["region"],
        "secondary_hq_continent": None,
        "is_financial_sector": firms["is_financial_sector"].astype(bool),
    })
    # Build annual 1 January snapshot dates 2004-2025 (item viii).
    snapshots: pd.DatetimeIndex = pd.date_range(
        "2004-01-01", SECOND_ANCHOR, freq="YS"
    )
    # Cross every firm with every snapshot date.
    membership: pd.DataFrame = pd.MultiIndex.from_product(
        [firms["firm_id"], snapshots], names=["firm_id", "as_of_date"]
    ).to_frame(index=False)

    leaver: pd.Series = membership["firm_id"].map(
        firms.set_index("firm_id")["is_leaver"]
    )

    membership["is_constituent"] = ~(
        leaver & (membership["as_of_date"] >= "2023-01-01")
    )
    # Record the tracked index.
    membership["index_name"] = "MSCI World"
    # Record the snapshot vendor.
    membership["vendor"] = "MSCI_direct"
    # Announcements precede the effective date by two weeks.
    membership["announcement_date"] = (
        membership["as_of_date"] - pd.Timedelta(days=14)
    )
    # The membership state takes effect on the snapshot date.
    membership["effective_date"] = membership["as_of_date"]
    # Return both tables.
    return metadata, membership


def build_fundamentals_and_segments(
    rng: np.random.Generator,
    firms: pd.DataFrame,
    df_returns: pd.DataFrame,
    fx_wide: pd.DataFrame,
    label_mix: Mapping[str, Mapping[str, float]],
) -> Tuple[pd.DataFrame, pd.DataFrame]:
    """
    Build ``df_firm_fundamentals_raw`` and ``df_segment_revenue_raw``.

    Tangible assets follow Equation (11)'s numerator: sector-average
    asset intensity (Real Estate 10.6, Retailers 0.6, Section 3.1) x a
    regional multiplier x revenue x lognormal dispersion. Fiscal years
    end in March (JPY filers), June (AUD/NZD) or December; values are
    converted at the FYE-month USD rate; segments cover 97.5-100% of
    revenue (inside the +/-5% reconciliation tolerance).

    Parameters
    ----------
    rng : np.random.Generator
        Seeded generator.
    firms : pd.DataFrame
        Firm skeleton.
    df_returns : pd.DataFrame
        Monthly panel carrying market caps (revenue anchor).
    fx_wide : pd.DataFrame
        Month x currency USD-per-unit rates.
    label_mix : Mapping[str, Mapping[str, float]]
        Firm -> segment label -> revenue share.

    Returns
    -------
    Tuple[pd.DataFrame, pd.DataFrame]
        (fundamentals, segment revenue), FY2015-FY2024.
    """
    # Collect fundamentals rows.
    fund_rows: List[Dict[str, Any]] = []
    # Collect segment rows.
    seg_rows: List[Dict[str, Any]] = []
    # Index caps by (firm, month) for FYE lookups.
    cap_lookup: pd.Series = df_returns.set_index(
        ["firm_id", "year_month"]
    )["market_cap_usd"]
    # Build each firm's fiscal years.
    for firm in firms.itertuples(index=False):
        # Japanese filers close in March, Australasian filers in June.
        fye_month: int = 3 if firm.currency == "JPY" else (
            6 if firm.currency in ("AUD", "NZD") else 12)
        # Draw a stable price-to-sales multiple for the firm.
        price_to_sales: float = float(np.exp(rng.normal(0.7, 0.4)))
        # Label the accounting regime (US filers report under US GAAP).
        standard: str = "US_GAAP" if firm.country == "US" else "IFRS"
        # Label the segment disclosure standard accordingly.
        disclosure: str = "ASC_280" if firm.country == "US" else "IFRS_8"
        # Walk the ten fiscal years.
        for fy in range(2015, 2025):
            # Fiscal-year-end date (the fiscal year is labelled by its end).
            fye: pd.Timestamp = (
                pd.Timestamp(year=fy, month=fye_month, day=1)
                + pd.offsets.MonthEnd(0)
            )
            # Month-start key of the FYE month.
            fye_key: pd.Timestamp = fye.to_period("M").to_timestamp()
            # USD per local unit at the FYE month.
            fx_fye: float = float(fx_wide.loc[fye_key, firm.currency])
            # Revenue = market cap at FYE / price-to-sales multiple.
            revenue_usd: float = (
                float(cap_lookup[(firm.firm_id, fye_key)]) / price_to_sales
            )
            # Tangible assets = sector AI x regional multiplier x revenue
            # x lognormal dispersion (Eq. 11 numerator).
            tangible_usd: float = (
                SECTOR_ASSET_INTENSITY[firm.sector]
                * REGION_AI_MULTIPLIER[firm.region]
                * revenue_usd * float(np.exp(rng.normal(0.0, 0.25)))
            )
            # Annual reports are filed about 75 days after the FYE.
            effective: pd.Timestamp = fye + pd.Timedelta(days=75)
            # Record the fundamentals row (item v).
            fund_rows.append({
                "firm_id": firm.firm_id, "fiscal_year": fy,
                "fiscal_year_end_date": fye,
                "tangible_assets_usd": tangible_usd,
                "tangible_assets_original_currency": tangible_usd / fx_fye,
                "revenue_usd": revenue_usd,
                "revenue_original_currency": revenue_usd / fx_fye,
                "reporting_currency": firm.currency,
                "fx_rate_used": fx_fye, "fx_rate_date": fye,
                "accounting_standard": standard,
                "tangible_assets_interpretation": "net_tangible",
                "is_restated": False,
                "restatement_effective_date": pd.NaT,
                "effective_date": effective,
            })
            # Allocated segment revenue covers 97.5-100% of total revenue.
            coverage: float = float(rng.uniform(0.975, 1.0))
            # Record one segment row per reported label (item vi).
            for label, share in label_mix[firm.firm_id].items():
                # Segment revenue in USD.
                seg_usd: float = revenue_usd * coverage * share
                # Append the segment disclosure row.
                seg_rows.append({
                    "firm_id": firm.firm_id, "fiscal_year": fy,
                    "segment_label": label,
                    "segment_revenue_usd": seg_usd,
                    "segment_revenue_original_currency": seg_usd / fx_fye,
                    "reporting_currency": firm.currency,
                    "fx_rate_used": fx_fye, "fx_rate_date": fye,
                    "disclosure_standard": disclosure,
                    "is_unallocated": False, "is_restated": False,
                    "effective_date": effective,
                })
    # Return both tables.
    return pd.DataFrame(fund_rows), pd.DataFrame(seg_rows)


def bridge_daily_returns(
    rng: np.random.Generator,
    firms: pd.DataFrame,
    universe: pd.DataFrame,
    df_returns: pd.DataFrame,
    df_calendar: pd.DataFrame,
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Build the daily total-return panel of the 120 universe firms.

    The specification lists no daily table, but Tasks 28-29, 31 and
    35 estimate Sigma from five years of DAILY returns (Section 4.2).
    Each monthly USD return is split across the month's trading days
    with a Brownian bridge on log returns, d_j = g/n + (z_j - mean z),
    with z_j = beta_i m_j + e_ij, so that prod_j (1 + r_j) = 1 + R_month
    exactly and daily returns co-move through the market shock m_j.

    Parameters
    ----------
    rng : np.random.Generator
        Seeded generator.
    firms : pd.DataFrame
        Firm skeleton (beta).
    universe : pd.DataFrame
        Frozen universe (firm_id).
    df_returns : pd.DataFrame
        Monthly panel (total_return_usd).
    df_calendar : pd.DataFrame
        Trading calendar (is_trading_day).

    Returns
    -------
    pd.DataFrame
        Long panel (firm_id, date, total_return_usd), 2015-01..2025-04.
    """
    # Trading days of the global-intersection calendar.
    trading_days: pd.DatetimeIndex = pd.DatetimeIndex(
        df_calendar.loc[df_calendar["is_trading_day"], "date"]
    )
    # Months of the daily panel (five-year lookback before 2020-01).
    months: pd.DatetimeIndex = _month_grid("2015-01-01", RETURNS_END)
    # Index the monthly USD returns by (firm, month).
    r_lookup: pd.Series = df_returns.set_index(
        ["firm_id", "year_month"]
    )["total_return_usd"]
    # Pre-group trading days by month.
    days_by_month: Dict[pd.Timestamp, pd.DatetimeIndex] = {
        m: trading_days[trading_days.to_period("M") == m.to_period("M")]
        for m in months
    }
    # Draw one common daily market shock path (0.9% daily volatility).
    market_daily: Dict[pd.Timestamp, np.ndarray] = {
        m: rng.normal(0.0, 0.009, days_by_month[m].size) for m in months
    }
    # Look up each firm's market beta.
    beta_of: pd.Series = firms.set_index("firm_id")["beta"]
    # Collect one frame per universe firm.
    frames: List[pd.DataFrame] = []
    # Bridge every universe firm's monthly return into daily returns.
    for fid in universe["firm_id"]:
        # Collect this firm's trading days month by month.
        firm_days: List[pd.DatetimeIndex] = []
        # Collect the matching daily simple returns.
        firm_rets: List[np.ndarray] = []
        # Walk the months of the daily window.
        for m in months:
            # Trading days of the month.
            days: pd.DatetimeIndex = days_by_month[m]
            # Monthly log return g = log(1 + R_month).
            g: float = math.log1p(float(r_lookup[(fid, m)]))
            # Daily shock: beta x market shock + idiosyncratic (1.1%).
            z: np.ndarray = (
                float(beta_of[fid]) * market_daily[m]
                + rng.normal(0.0, 0.011, days.size)
            )
            # Brownian bridge: d_j = g/n + (z_j - mean z), sum_j d_j = g.
            d: np.ndarray = g / days.size + (z - z.mean())
            # Store the month's days.
            firm_days.append(days)
            # Convert daily log returns to simple returns.
            firm_rets.append(np.expm1(d))
        # Assemble the firm's daily frame.
        frames.append(pd.DataFrame({
            "firm_id": fid,
            "date": np.concatenate([d.values for d in firm_days]),
            "total_return_usd": np.concatenate(firm_rets),
        }))
    # Stack the daily panel.
    return pd.concat(frames, ignore_index=True)


def generate_study_inputs(
    seed: int = SYNTHETIC_SEED,
) -> Dict[str, pd.DataFrame]:
    """
    Generate the twelve raw tables of the revised input specification
    plus the daily total-return panel.

    Purpose
    -------
    Produces one internally consistent synthetic world: firm returns
    load on the market factor (Eq. 8) and on the realised extreme-event
    indicators B_k,t of the synthetic temperature panel through each
    firm's true continental revenue exposure S_i,k (Eq. 12); FX,
    fundamentals, segments, membership and the frozen 120-stock
    universe all agree with one another.

    Processes
    ---------
    temperature -> B_k,t -> firm skeleton -> FX -> market -> risk-free
    -> segment mixes -> returns -> universe -> metadata/membership ->
    fundamentals/segments -> calendar -> daily bridge.

    Parameters
    ----------
    seed : int, optional
        Master seed of the data-generating process.

    Returns
    -------
    Dict[str, pd.DataFrame]
        The 12 specification tables keyed by their specification names
        plus ``df_daily_total_returns_raw``.

    Raises
    ------
    TypeError
        If ``seed`` is not an int.
    """
    # Validate the seed type (bool is rejected explicitly).
    if not isinstance(seed, int) or isinstance(seed, bool):
        # Raise naming the received type.
        raise TypeError(f"seed must be an int, got {type(seed).__name__}.")
    # Create the single PCG64 stream driving the whole world.
    rng: np.random.Generator = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    # Generate the temperature panel (item i).
    df_temperature: pd.DataFrame = generate_temperature_panel(rng)
    # Recover the realised B_k,t used by the return DGP.
    b_wide: pd.DataFrame = simulate_bernoulli_events(df_temperature)
    # Generate the static firm attributes.
    firms: pd.DataFrame = generate_firm_skeleton(rng)
    # Generate the FX table (item xi).
    df_fx: pd.DataFrame = generate_fx_rates(rng)
    # Generate the market factor (item iii).
    df_mkt: pd.DataFrame = generate_market_factor(rng)
    # Generate the risk-free series (item iv).
    df_rf: pd.DataFrame = generate_riskfree()
    # Pivot FX to a month x currency matrix over the return window.
    fx_wide: pd.DataFrame = df_fx.pivot(
        index="year_month", columns="currency", values="rate"
    ).reindex(_month_grid(RETURNS_START, RETURNS_END))
    # USD is the numeraire: one USD per USD.
    fx_wide["USD"] = 1.0
    # Draw each firm's reporting-label revenue mix.
    label_mix: Dict[str, Dict[str, float]] = {
        fid: draw_label_shares(rng, region)
        for fid, region in zip(firms["firm_id"], firms["region"])
    }
    # Convert every mix to the true continental exposure S_i,k.
    s_true: pd.DataFrame = pd.DataFrame(
        {fid: label_shares_to_continents(m) for fid, m in label_mix.items()},
        index=list(CONTINENTS),
    ).T
    # Simulate the monthly firm return panel (item ii).
    df_returns: pd.DataFrame = simulate_firm_returns(
        rng, firms, b_wide, s_true, df_mkt, df_rf, fx_wide
    )
    # Freeze the 120-firm universe (item ix).
    df_universe: pd.DataFrame = freeze_universe(firms, df_returns)
    # Build metadata and membership (items vii-viii).
    df_metadata, df_membership = build_metadata_and_membership(firms)
    # Build fundamentals and segment revenue (items v-vi).
    df_fundamentals, df_segments = build_fundamentals_and_segments(
        rng, firms, df_returns, fx_wide, label_mix
    )
    # Generate the trading calendar (item xii).
    df_calendar: pd.DataFrame = generate_trading_calendar()
    # Bridge monthly returns into the daily panel.
    df_daily: pd.DataFrame = bridge_daily_returns(
        rng, firms, df_universe, df_returns, df_calendar
    )
    # Return every table under its specification name.
    return {
        "df_temperature_raw": df_temperature,
        "df_firm_total_returns_raw": df_returns,
        "df_market_factor_raw": df_mkt,
        "df_riskfree_raw": df_rf,
        "df_firm_fundamentals_raw": df_fundamentals,
        "df_segment_revenue_raw": df_segments,
        "df_firm_metadata_raw": df_metadata,
        "df_index_membership_raw": df_membership,
        "df_universe_120_raw": df_universe,
        "df_segment_to_continent_map_raw": generate_segment_map(),
        "df_fx_rates_raw": df_fx,
        "df_trading_calendar_raw": df_calendar,
        "df_daily_total_returns_raw": df_daily,
    }
```

### **Step 2: Loading the Configuration (`config.yaml`)**

**Methodology.**
1. Open `config.yaml` from the working directory as UTF-8.
2. Parse it with `yaml.safe_load`, which never constructs arbitrary Python objects.
3. Verify, *before* any stage runs, the structure the Task 1 validator enforces: a mapping with **exactly** the 21 required sections, and the stored `config_checksum_sha256`.
4. Keep the result as a plain `dict` named `config`. Task 1 requires a genuine `dict`, and `run_research_pipeline` injects a deep copy of it into every stage that accepts `config`.

```python
# ==========================================================================
# Step 2: load config.yaml into the ``config`` dictionary
# ==========================================================================


def load_study_configuration(filepath: str = "config.yaml") -> Dict[str, Any]:
    """
    Load the study configuration from YAML into a plain nested dict.

    Purpose
    -------
    Reads the 21-section STUDY_CONFIG that ``run_research_pipeline``
    shares with every stage and verifies, before any stage runs, the
    structural facts the Task 1 validator will later enforce.

    Processes
    ---------
    1. Validate the path argument and the file's existence.
    2. Parse with ``yaml.safe_load`` (no arbitrary object construction).
    3. Verify a mapping with exactly the 21 required top-level sections.
    4. Verify the stored configuration checksum is present.

    Parameters
    ----------
    filepath : str, optional
        Path of the YAML file, relative to the working directory.

    Returns
    -------
    Dict[str, Any]
        The configuration as a plain ``dict`` (Task 1 requires a
        genuine dict, not merely a Mapping).

    Raises
    ------
    TypeError
        If ``filepath`` is not a string.
    FileNotFoundError
        If the file does not exist.
    yaml.YAMLError
        If the file is not valid YAML.
    ValueError
        If the document is not a mapping or its sections are wrong.
    """
    # Validate the argument type.
    if not isinstance(filepath, str):
        # Raise naming the received type.
        raise TypeError(
            f"filepath must be a str, got {type(filepath).__name__}."
        )
    # Resolve the path relative to the working directory.
    path: Path = Path(filepath)
    # Refuse to continue silently when the file is missing.
    if not path.is_file():
        # Raise with the absolute path for diagnosis.
        raise FileNotFoundError(f"configuration not found: {path.resolve()}")
    # Open the file as UTF-8 text.
    with path.open("r", encoding="utf-8") as handle:
        # Parse safely; YAMLError propagates on malformed input.
        loaded: Any = yaml.safe_load(handle)
    # Require a mapping at the document root.
    if not isinstance(loaded, dict):
        # Raise naming the parsed type.
        raise ValueError(
            f"config root must be a mapping, got {type(loaded).__name__}."
        )
    # Declare the 21 sections the Task 1 validator requires, no more.
    required_sections: Tuple[str, ...] = (
        "study_metadata", "global_design_constants", "raw_input_schemas",
        "data_preprocessing_parameters", "module_M1_climate_signal",
        "module_M2_dependence", "module_M3_regressions", "module_M5_metrics",
        "module_M6_optimization", "module_M7_scalarization",
        "module_M8_backtest", "mopso_hyperparameters",
        "diagnostics_and_reporting", "plot_construction",
        "prompt_templates", "llm_configuration",
        "network_architecture_parameters", "network_training_parameters",
        "explicitly_absent_components", "methodological_notes",
        "coverage_verification",
    )
    # Compute missing and unexpected sections.
    missing: List[str] = [s for s in required_sections if s not in loaded]
    # Any extra top-level key is rejected by Task 1 as well.
    extra: List[str] = [s for s in loaded if s not in required_sections]
    # Abort before the pipeline when the structure is wrong.
    if missing or extra:

        raise ValueError(
            f"config sections mismatch: missing={missing} extra={extra}"
        )
    # Require the canonical checksum recorded under study_metadata.
    if "config_checksum_sha256" not in loaded["study_metadata"]:
        # Raise because Task 1 recomputes and records this digest.
        raise ValueError("study_metadata.config_checksum_sha256 is absent.")
    # Log a concise confirmation.
    example_logger.info(
        "Loaded %d configuration sections from %s.", len(loaded), path
    )
    # Return the plain dictionary.
    return loaded
```

### **Step 3: Executing the Pipeline (`run_research_pipeline`)**

**Methodology.**
1. **One call per stage.** `run_stage` wraps `run_research_pipeline(stage_inputs={N: kwargs}, config=config, out_dir=..., stages=[N], fail_fast=True)`, persists that call's `pipeline_manifest.json` under `study_run/manifests/task_N/`, and returns the stage deliverable `outcome["stages"]["task_N"]`.
2. **Configuration-driven optimisation.** Algorithm 1's hyperparameters are read from `config["mopso_hyperparameters"]` (Table C.10: $`N_{pop}=500`$, $`Iter=300`$, $`N_{rep}=400`$, $`N_{grid}=10`$, $`\alpha=0.1`$, $`\beta=\gamma=1`$, $`\mu=2`$, $`\omega=0.8`$, $`c_1=c_2=1.5`$) through the notebook's own `REQUIRED_MOPSO_KEYS`.
3. **Runtime.** At the paper's settings, the anchor optimisation takes minutes, the 27-cell × 10-seed sweep of Tables 5–7 takes many hours (the manuscript benchmarks 217.39 s per run), and the backtest re-optimises every month from 2020-01 to 2025-04. Setting the environment variable `CLIMATE_RISK_SMOKE_TEST=1` shrinks only the swarm ($`N_{pop}=30`$, $`Iter=15`$, $`N_{rep}=30`$) and the sweep grid (2 cells × 2 seeds) for a fast wiring check. **The default is the paper's settings.**

```python
def build_table9_reference_frame(

def render_figure_c13(pareto_objectives: np.ndarray, out_path: Path) -> Path:
    """
    Render Figure C.13: three perspectives of the 3-D Pareto frontier.

    The notebook contains no Figure C.13 renderer, so the example draws
    it from Task 31's anchor archive: expected return (the negated
    first objective), market variance and CEV (Equations 19a-19c).

    Parameters
    ----------
    pareto_objectives : np.ndarray, shape (M, 3)
        Archive rows (-E[R], variance, CEV).
    out_path : Path
        Destination PNG.

    Returns
    -------
    Path
        The written figure.

    Raises
    ------
    ValueError
        If the objective matrix is not (M, 3) with M >= 1.
    """
    # Coerce to float and validate the (M, 3) shape.
    obj: np.ndarray = np.asarray(pareto_objectives, dtype=float)
    # Reject a malformed archive.
    if obj.ndim != 2 or obj.shape[1] != 3 or obj.shape[0] < 1:
        # Raise naming the observed shape.
        raise ValueError(f"expected (M, 3) objectives, got {obj.shape}.")
    # Create one figure with three 3-D panels.
    fig = plt.figure(figsize=(15.0, 5.0))
    # Draw the frontier from three viewing angles.
    views: Tuple[Tuple[int, int], ...] = ((25, 45), (20, 135), (60, 225))
    # Draw each perspective in its own panel.
    for i, (elev, azim) in enumerate(views, start=1):
        # Add a 3-D axis for this perspective.
        ax = fig.add_subplot(1, 3, i, projection="3d")
        # Plot variance, CEV and expected return (= -f1).
        ax.scatter(obj[:, 1], obj[:, 2], -obj[:, 0], c=obj[:, 2], s=12)
        # Set the viewing angle.
        ax.view_init(elev=elev, azim=azim)
        # Label the axes with the Equation (19a) objectives.
        ax.set_xlabel("Market variance")
        # Label the CEV axis.
        ax.set_ylabel("CEV")
        # Label the expected-return axis.
        ax.set_zlabel("Expected return")
        # Title the panel.
        ax.set_title(f"Perspective {i}")
    # Create the destination directory.
    out_path.parent.mkdir(parents=True, exist_ok=True)
    # Save the figure.
    fig.savefig(out_path, dpi=150, bbox_inches="tight")
    # Release the figure's memory.
    plt.close(fig)
    # Return the written path.
    return out_path
```

```python
def run_stage(
    stage: int,
    stage_kwargs: Mapping[str, Any],
    study_config: Mapping[str, Any],
) -> Dict[str, Any]:
    """
    Run one pipeline stage through ``run_research_pipeline``.

    Purpose
    -------
    ``run_research_pipeline`` executes the requested stages with the
    keyword arguments supplied up front; it does not forward one
    stage's outputs into the next stage. This wrapper therefore runs
    ONE stage per call, persists that call's run manifest, and returns
    the stage's artifact mapping so the caller can build the next
    stage's inputs from it.

    Parameters
    ----------
    stage : int
        Study task number in [1, 39], excluding engine-only Task 30.
    stage_kwargs : Mapping[str, Any]
        Keyword arguments of ``orchestrate_task_<stage>``.
    study_config : Mapping[str, Any]
        Shared configuration (injected when the stage accepts it).

    Returns
    -------
    Dict[str, Any]
        The stage's own deliverable mapping.

    Raises
    ------
    TypeError
        If ``stage`` is not an int or ``stage_kwargs`` is not a mapping.
    RuntimeError
        If the stage raises (fail-fast, with the cause chained).
    """
    # Validate the stage number type (bool is rejected explicitly).
    if not isinstance(stage, int) or isinstance(stage, bool):
        # Raise naming the received type.
        raise TypeError(f"stage must be int, got {type(stage).__name__}.")
    # Validate the keyword-argument container.
    if not isinstance(stage_kwargs, Mapping):
        # Raise naming the received type.
        raise TypeError("stage_kwargs must be a Mapping.")
    # Execute exactly this stage, fail-fast, persisting its manifest.
    outcome: Dict[str, Any] = run_research_pipeline(
        stage_inputs={stage: dict(stage_kwargs)},
        config=study_config,
        out_dir=str(MANIFEST_DIR / f"task_{stage}"),
        stages=[stage],
        fail_fast=True,
    )
    # Extract the stage's deliverable by its canonical label.
    artifacts: Dict[str, Any] = outcome["stages"][f"task_{stage}"]
    # Read the stage's own verdict when it reports one.
    verdict: Any = artifacts.get("overall_passed", artifacts.get("passed"))
    # Log the wall time and verdict of the stage.
    example_logger.info(
        "task_%d finished in %.2fs; stage verdict=%s",
        stage,
        outcome["manifest"]["timings_seconds"][f"task_{stage}"],
        verdict,
    )
    # Return the stage deliverable.
    return artifacts
```

Stage 39 does four things:
- copies the 25 canonical artifacts (10 tables and 15 figures) under canonical names;

- writes `environment.json` and a README;
- archives everything as `reproducibility_bundle.tar.gz`.

`study_run/` is the report directory, because the bundler looks there for `logs/hyperparameter_sweep.jsonl` and `configs/selected_mopso_config.json`.

> **Task sources come from the notebook.** Stage 39's README task registry (`build_task_registry`) and its judgment-call register gate (`judgment_call_reconciliation`) parse each task's `# Task N:` code cell in the notebook file, via Task 1's `load_task_sources`.
```python
# ==========================================================================
# Step 3 (continued): Stage 39, the final report and reproducibility bundle
# ==========================================================================


def build_report_inventory(
    artifacts_by_task: Mapping[str, Mapping[str, Any]],
    table_8_path: Path,
    figure_c13_path: Path,
) -> Dict[str, Path]:
    """
    Map Task 39's 25 canonical report names onto the files Stages 1-38 wrote.

    Parameters
    ----------
    artifacts_by_task : Mapping[str, Mapping[str, Any]]
        Stage deliverables keyed ``task_N``.
    table_8_path : Path
        Table 8 file written by :func:`write_table8`.
    figure_c13_path : Path
        Figure C.13 file written by :func:`render_figure_c13`.

    Returns
    -------
    Dict[str, Path]
        Canonical name -> existing source file.

    Raises
    ------
    FileNotFoundError
        Naming every canonical artifact that is absent, e.g. Tables
        5-7 and C.10, which Task 34 writes only for the full 27-cell
        sweep.
    """
    # Shorten the lookup of each stage's path mapping.
    p = {k: v.get("paths", {}) for k, v in artifacts_by_task.items()}
    # Declare the source of every canonical table and figure.
    inventory: Dict[str, Any] = {
        "table_1": p["task_8"].get("table_1"),
        "table_2": p["task_18"].get("table_2"),
        "table_3": p["task_20"].get("table_3"),
        "table_4": p["task_14"].get("frozen_universe_canonical"),
        "table_5": p["task_34"].get("table_5"),
        "table_6": p["task_34"].get("table_6"),
        "table_7": p["task_34"].get("table_7"),
        "table_8": table_8_path,
        "table_9": artifacts_by_task["task_36"]["artifacts"].get("table_9"),
        "table_c10": p["task_34"].get("selected_config"),
        "figure_1": p["task_8"].get("figure_1"),
        "figure_2": p["task_10"].get("figure_2"),
        "figure_3": p["task_11"].get("figure_3"),
        "figure_4": p["task_13"].get("figure_4"),
        "figure_5": p["task_26"].get("figure_5"),
        "figure_6": p["task_27"].get("figure_6"),
        "figure_7": artifacts_by_task["task_31"]["figure"]["figure_path"],
        "figure_8a": p["task_32"].get("figure_8a"),
        "figure_8b": p["task_32"].get("figure_8b"),
        "figure_9": p["task_32"].get("slices"),
        "figure_10": artifacts_by_task["task_36"].get("figure_10"),
        "figure_B11a": artifacts_by_task["task_38"].get("figure_B11a"),
        "figure_B11b": artifacts_by_task["task_38"].get("figure_B11b"),
        "figure_B12": artifacts_by_task["task_38"].get("figure_B12"),
        "figure_C13": figure_c13_path,
    }
    # Collect every canonical name whose file does not exist.
    missing: List[str] = [
        name for name, src in inventory.items()
        if src is None or not Path(str(src)).is_file()
    ]
    # Refuse to hand Task 39 an incomplete inventory.
    if missing:
        # Raise naming every absent artifact.
        raise FileNotFoundError(f"report artifacts absent: {missing}")
    # Return the inventory with Path values.
    return {name: Path(str(src)) for name, src in inventory.items()}


def build_verification_inputs(
    study_config: Mapping[str, Any],
    artifacts_by_task: Mapping[str, Mapping[str, Any]],
    params_used: Mapping[str, Any],
    n_figures: int,
) -> Tuple[pd.DataFrame, pd.DataFrame, List[str]]:
    """
    Build Task 39's ``reproduced``/``references`` frames and label list.

    The 22 ``Equation_*`` labels are verified by Task 39's own analytic
    fixtures; the ten named-artifact labels need one reproduced and one
    reference scalar each. This example uses explicit, auditable
    structural quantities, each compared with the value the
    configuration prescribes:

    - Algorithm_1_MOPSO: anchor archive size |rep| vs N_rep used.
    - Table_04_universe: frozen universe size vs
      universe_construction.size (120).
    - Table_08_tilts: tilts resolved by the backtest vs
      len(tilt_configurations.tilts) (6).
    - Table_09_performance_metrics: strategies in Table 9 vs
      len(module_M8_backtest.strategy_roster) (11).
    - Table_C_10_mopso_config: N_pop used vs
      selected_configuration.N_pop (500).
    - Ledoit_Wolf_shrinkage: 1{cond(Sigma_tau) < 1000} vs 1.
    - Backtest_rolling_window: rebalance months executed vs months in
      backtest_window (64).
    - Diagnostics_reporting: continents in Table 1 vs 6.
    - Plot_construction: canonical figures produced vs 15.
    - Absence_declarations: absent components declared vs 4.

    Parameters
    ----------
    study_config : Mapping[str, Any]
        The loaded configuration.
    artifacts_by_task : Mapping[str, Mapping[str, Any]]
        Stage deliverables keyed ``task_N``.
    params_used : Mapping[str, Any]
        MOPSO hyperparameters the anchor run actually used.
    n_figures : int
        Canonical figures present in the report inventory.

    Returns
    -------
    Tuple[pd.DataFrame, pd.DataFrame, List[str]]
        Name-indexed ``value`` frames and the 32 coverage labels.
    """
    # Read the coverage map: its keys are the labels Task 39 requires.
    required_names: List[str] = sorted(study_config["coverage_verification"])
    # Read the months of the configured backtest window.
    window: Mapping[str, Any] = (
        study_config["module_M8_backtest"]["backtest_window"]
    )
    # Count the inclusive month grid of the window.
    n_window_months: int = len(
        pd.date_range(window["start"], window["end"], freq="MS")
    )
    # Read the ceiling of the Section 4.2 condition-number benchmark.
    ceiling: float = float(study_config["module_M6_optimization"][
        "covariance_estimation"]["condition_number_ceiling"])
    # Read the absent-component declarations.
    absent_sections: Tuple[str, ...] = (
        "prompt_templates", "llm_configuration",
        "network_architecture_parameters", "network_training_parameters",
    )
    # Pair each artifact label with (reproduced, reference).
    pairs: Dict[str, Tuple[float, float]] = {
        "Algorithm_1_MOPSO": (
            float(np.asarray(artifacts_by_task["task_31"]["execution"][
                "result"]["pareto_weights"]).shape[0]),
            float(params_used["N_rep"])),
        "Table_04_universe": (
            float(len(artifacts_by_task["task_14"]["order"])),
            float(study_config["module_M6_optimization"][
                "universe_construction"]["size"])),
        "Table_08_tilts": (
            float(len(resolve_table8_tilts(dict(study_config)))),
            float(len(study_config["module_M7_scalarization"][
                "tilt_configurations"]["tilts"]))),
        "Table_09_performance_metrics": (
            float(len(artifacts_by_task["task_36"]["table_9"])),
            float(len(study_config["module_M8_backtest"]["strategy_roster"]))),
        "Table_C_10_mopso_config": (
            float(params_used["N_pop"]),
            float(study_config["mopso_hyperparameters"][
                "selected_configuration"]["N_pop"])),
        "Ledoit_Wolf_shrinkage": (
            float(artifacts_by_task["task_29"]["condition"][
                "condition_number"] < ceiling),
            1.0),
        "Backtest_rolling_window": (
            float(len(artifacts_by_task["task_36"]["rebalance_dates"])),
            float(n_window_months)),
        "Diagnostics_reporting": (
            float(artifacts_by_task["task_8"]["auroc"]["continent"].nunique()),
            float(len(CONTINENTS))),
        "Plot_construction": (float(n_figures), 15.0),
        "Absence_declarations": (
            float(sum(not bool(study_config[s].get("present", False))
                      for s in absent_sections)),
            float(len(absent_sections))),
    }
    # Build the reproduced frame (name-indexed ``value`` column).
    reproduced: pd.DataFrame = pd.DataFrame(
        {"value": {k: v[0] for k, v in pairs.items()}})
    # Build the reference frame (name-indexed ``value`` column).
    references: pd.DataFrame = pd.DataFrame(
        {"value": {k: v[1] for k, v in pairs.items()}})
    # Return both frames and the full coverage label list.
    return reproduced, references, required_names
```

### **Summary of the Execution Flow**

1. **Assumptions.** All callables live in one executed notebook, with no `.py` task folder. `config.yaml` is in the working directory, which is the notebook's own folder. Every artifact goes under `./study_run/`.
2. **Synthetic data (Step 1).** One seeded world produces the 12 specified raw tables plus the daily return panel:
   - temperatures carry a warming trend;
   - returns load on the realised $`B_{k,t}`$ through true revenue exposures (Equation 8);
   - the 120-firm universe, fundamentals, segments, FX, membership and calendar are mutually consistent;
   - controlled vocabularies and alias columns match the notebook's validators.
3. **Configuration (Step 2).** `config.yaml` is read with `yaml.safe_load` into the plain `dict` named `config`, after checking its 21 sections and checksum.
4. **Interface semantics.** `run_research_pipeline` validates, deep-copies, injects `config`, executes, times and writes a manifest, but it does not chain outputs between stages. The example therefore calls it once per stage in canonical order, feeding each stage's deliverable into the next.
5. **Climate signal (Stages 1–13).** Validation and cleansing come first. Then come the baseline moments, $`Z_{k,t}`$ (Equation 1), $`B_{k,t}`$ (Equation 2), the quadratic-trend logit $`\hat p_{k,t}`$ (Equation 3) with AUROC/AUPR (Table 1), the empirical correlations, $`\bar p_k`$ (Equation 7) and the Fréchet–Hoeffding bounds (Equations 5–6).
6. **Econometrics (Stages 14–21).** These produce the survivorship-controlled universe (Table 4), $`AI_i`$ (Equation 11) and $`S_{i,k}`$, and the excess returns. Equations (8)–(10) are estimated under a shared two-scope `run_id`, giving Tables 2–3 and $`\Gamma`$.
7. **Climate metrics (Stages 22–27).** Stage 22 computes $`\alpha_k`$ (Equation 12). Stages 23–27 compute CRE and CEV (Equations 13–16), the asymptotic limits (Equations 17–18), the strategy comparison (Figure 5, recorded with `report_only`) and the correlation stress scenarios (Figure 6).
8. **Optimisation (Stages 28–34, 37–38).** Stages 28–29 give the rolling $`\mathbf{r}_\tau`$ and the Ledoit–Wolf $`\Sigma_\tau`$. Stage 31 solves the anchor three-objective MOPSO (Equations 19a–19c, Algorithm 1, Figure 7). Stage 32 produces the frontier diagnostics (Figures 8–9), Stages 33–34 the hyperparameter sweep (Tables 5–7, C.10), and Stages 37–38 the Appendix B CRE variant (Figures B.11–B.12).
9. **Backtest (Stages 35–36).** There are 64 monthly re-optimisations of 11 strategies, producing Table 9, compared cell by cell with the manuscript, and Figure 10, gross of costs.
10. **Reporting (Stage 39).** This stage produces the canonical report, the equation and artifact verification, the environment record, a README whose task registry is derived from the notebook's own task cells, and the `reproducibility_bundle.tar.gz` archive. It needs the full sweep for Tables 5–7 and C.10.
11. **Verification.** Every code block of this document was executed in order against the notebook, in a sandboxed directory that contained only the notebook and `config.yaml`, with `CLIMATE_RISK_SMOKE_TEST=1`. Stages 1–38 completed, and Stage 39 stopped as documented for smoke mode because Tables 5–7 and C.10 were absent. In a second check, stand-in files were supplied for those four sweep outputs only. Stage 39 then completed, with `passed=True` and all six gates True (fixtures, coverage, readme, register, environment, bundle). Nothing was written outside `./study_run/`.

## Recommended Extensions

1. **Cross-platform use:** guard the `winreg` import so that the notebook runs on Linux and macOS.
2. **Real-data ingestion:** add a loader for licensed data together with a provenance gate that records source and snapshot dates.
3. **Country-level granularity:** use national temperature and revenue data, which the paper itself suggests.
4. **Other hazards:** floods, sea-level rise and other perils, given their Bernoulli time series and firm exposures.
5. **Higher-order dependence:** admissibility checks beyond pairwise Bernoulli bounds, and a time-varying $`\rho`$.
6. **Transaction costs:** cost-aware backtests with turnover penalties and bid–ask spreads.
7. **Uncertainty propagation:** carry the estimation error of $`\hat p_{k,t}`$ into CRE and CEV; the paper treats the probabilities as known.
8. **Conventional ratios:** a built-in option for $`\sqrt{12}`$ Sharpe and Sortino annualisation.
9. **Faster sweeps:** run the seeds of each sweep cell in parallel.
